kind: E3 slice 2a, `soft_f128` conversions (`to_f32`, `from_f32`, `to_i256`) and 1,259 vectors against an independent reference
---
`F128` gains `to_f32` (one rounding from the exact value, never through `f64`: a path through `f64` double-rounds a value a hair either side of an `f32` tie, which the vectors pin), `from_f32` (exact), and `to_i256(mode)` (the value rounded to an integer under any of the five rounding modes as a 256-bit two's-complement word, or `None` for a NaN, an infinity or a magnitude of 2^255 and above, for the caller to saturate). Additive: new methods, nothing existing changes, `F128` is still not a kernel dtype (`check_kernel` keeps declining it until the oracle runs it end to end: slice 2b wires Load, Store, arithmetic, compare, `Select` and `Cast` and flips that in one commit).

`tests/soft_f128_vectors.rs` checks every operation against `tests/data/soft_f128_vectors.txt`, produced by `tools/gen_f128_vectors.py`, a separate implementation (exact rational arithmetic, round to nearest even, sharing no code with `soft_f128.rs`): add, sub, mul and div over every pair of 10 specials (zeros, subnormals, the largest finite value, infinities, NaN) and 90 seeded random pairs from every binade region (ties and near-cancellation included), `tof64`, `tof32` (with 80 rows a hair either side of an `f32` tie), `fromf64`, `fromf32` and the five integer roundings. **UNVERIFIED against a third-party binary128 library** (none is installed here): the check is two independent implementations agreeing. Both agreed on the first run of the arithmetic rows; the 26 first differences in the conversion rows were the generator losing the sign of a zero, fixed there. 9 seeded mutants caught (a swapped or un-negated subtraction, the tie rule rounding up or down, the far-operand cutoff, a zero-sum sign, the `f32` path through `f64`, floor and ceil swapped, ties-away as ties-even, a sub-half remainder, a flipped `from_f32` sign).
